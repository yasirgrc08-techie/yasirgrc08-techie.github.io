# Behavioral Interviews and Project Stories / Practice Decisions

By Yasir Sharfi

## The Idea

What if I do not have a dramatic story?

A strong behavioral answer gives enough context, explains your decisions and actions, and reports an honest result. The goal is not to sound flawless. It is to make your judgment, learning, and collaboration understandable through specific evidence.

## Visual Blueprint

1. **Use structure as a guide**: Situation and task establish the problem; actions explain your reasoning; results and learning close the loop.
2. **Watch for**: Memorizing a polished script that collapses under follow-up questions.
3. **Prepare facts, not performances**: Record the constraints, alternatives, stakeholders, decision, evidence, and lesson. Practice a short version and a deeper version.
4. **Watch for**: Using 'we' so broadly that your personal contribution remains unclear.
5. **Show collaborative judgment**: Disagreement stories should explain competing goals and evidence, not label another person incompetent.
6. **Watch for**: Blaming colleagues or revealing sensitive incidents to make the story vivid.
