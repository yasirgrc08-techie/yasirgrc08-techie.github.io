⭐ The STAR Framework (Master this first)
S — Situation
Set the scene in 2-3 sentences. Where? When? What project? What was the scale/impact? Give enough context for the interviewer to follow.
T — Task
What was YOUR specific role/responsibility? What were the stakes? What was the deadline or expectation? Make it clear what YOU owned.
A — Action
This is 60% of your answer. What did YOU specifically do? Use "I" not "we". Be concrete — tools, techniques, conversations. Show your thought process.
R — Result
Quantify the outcome. "Reduced latency by 40%", "Saved 200 hours/year", "Zero incidents since". If no metrics, describe the behavioral change or process improvement.
Pro Tips for Behavioral Interviews

• Keep each story to 2-3 minutes max. Rambling is the #1 mistake.
• Prepare 10-15 stories that cover all themes (leadership, conflict, failure, teamwork, innovation).
• Each story can be reframed for multiple questions — a "failure" story can also show "learning" and "ownership".
• Practice out loud. Record yourself. Time yourself. Aim for concise, structured answers.
• Always end with what you LEARNED, not just what happened.
• Never badmouth colleagues, managers, or companies — even if they were terrible.

📋 Amazon Leadership Principles (All 16 — with interview tips)
1. Customer Obsession
Most important LP. Start with the customer and work backwards. Earn and keep customer trust. In interviews: show you prioritized user needs over engineering preferences. Example question: "Tell me about a time you went against the team's preference to do what was right for the customer."
2. Ownership
Think long term. Act on behalf of the entire company. Never say "that's not my job." In interviews: show you took responsibility for something outside your scope. Fixed a bug that wasn't yours. Owned a failing system nobody wanted.
3. Invent and Simplify
Seek new ideas from everywhere. Find ways to simplify. In interviews: show you built a tool/process that didn't exist. Replaced a complex system with a simpler one. Automated tedious manual work.
4. Are Right, A Lot
Strong judgment and good instincts. Seek diverse perspectives and work to disconfirm your beliefs. In interviews: show a time your judgment was correct despite pushback. Also show times you changed your mind based on new data.
5. Learn and Be Curious
Never done learning. Always seek to improve yourself. Explore new possibilities. In interviews: show self-directed learning — picked up a new language/framework, read papers, explored adjacent domains without being asked.
6. Hire and Develop the Best
Raise the performance bar with every hire. Recognize talent and help them grow. In interviews (SDE-2+): show mentoring impact, how you helped someone level up, or how you raised the team's bar through code reviews/standards.
7. Insist on Highest Standards
Continually raise the bar. Drive quality. Never accept "good enough." In interviews: show you pushed for better code quality, caught edge cases others missed, or refused to ship a subpar solution under time pressure.
8. Think Big
Create and communicate a bold direction. Think at 10x scale. In interviews: show a time you proposed a bigger vision — not just fixing the bug, but redesigning the system. Long-term thinking over quick patches.
9. Bias for Action
Speed matters. Many decisions are reversible — don't over-analyze. In interviews: show you made a decision with 70% data instead of waiting for 100%. Launched an MVP. Took calculated risks.
10. Frugality
Accomplish more with less. Constraints breed resourcefulness. In interviews: show you solved a problem without requesting more headcount, budget, or resources. Used open-source tools. Optimized existing systems.
11. Earn Trust
Listen attentively, speak candidly, treat others respectfully. Be self-critical. In interviews: show vulnerability — admit mistakes, give honest feedback respectfully, build credibility through actions not words.
12. Dive Deep
Operate at all levels. Stay connected to details. No task is beneath you. In interviews: show you debugged a production issue by reading logs line by line, or discovered a root cause others missed by going deeper.
13. Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
Challenge decisions respectfully when you disagree, even when it's uncomfortable. Once a decision is made, commit fully. In interviews: show you disagreed with data/logic (not emotion), AND show you committed even when overruled.
14. Deliver Results
Focus on the key inputs. Deliver with the right quality in a timely fashion. Rise to the occasion and never settle. In interviews: show you shipped despite obstacles — technical debt, changing requirements, resource constraints. Use metrics.
15. Strive to be Earth's Best Employer
(Newer LP, 2021) Create a safer, more productive, more diverse work environment. In interviews: show you advocated for team well-being, pushed back on unreasonable deadlines, or championed inclusion.
16. Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility
(Newer LP, 2021) Be humble. Think about the broader impact of your work. In interviews: show you considered sustainability, social impact, or ethical implications of a technical decision.
